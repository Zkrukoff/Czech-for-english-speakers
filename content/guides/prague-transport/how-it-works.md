## One system, one ticket

Prague's public transport is one integrated network called **PID** (*Pražská integrovaná doprava*). The same ticket works on the metro, trams, buses, trolleybuses, river ferries and, within the city, most trains (the Petřín funicular has its own rules, below). Most of it is run by **DPP** (*Dopravní podnik hl. m. Prahy*), the city transport company. Locals just call it all ***MHD*** (*městská hromadná doprava*, "city public transport").

| Czech | English | Lines are numbered… |
|---|---|---|
| **metro** | underground | A, B, C |
| **tramvaj** (*tramvaje*) | tram | 1–2 digits by day, **90–99** at night |
| **autobus** (*autobusy*) | bus | 3 digits by day, from **901** at night |
| **trolejbus** | trolleybus | e.g. **59** to the airport |
| **vlak** (*esko*) | suburban train | **S** + number (S1, S7…) |
| **přívoz** | river ferry | **P** + number |
| **lanovka** | funicular | the Petřín funicular |

## The metro

Three lines, each with its own colour. Czechs often call them by nicknames: ***áčko, béčko, céčko***.

| Line | Colour | Changes |
|---|---|---|
| **A** (*áčko*) | green | to B at **Můstek**, to C at **Muzeum** |
| **B** (*béčko*) | yellow | to A at **Můstek**, to C at **Florenc** |
| **C** (*céčko*) | red | to A at **Muzeum**, to B at **Florenc** |

A fourth line, **D**, is under construction.

Directions are shown by the **last station** of the line (*konečná*): to go from Můstek towards Prague Castle you take line A ***směr Nemocnice Motol***. Signs in stations say ***Směr*** ("towards"), then the end station.

The metro runs from about **5:00 to midnight**. Trains come every 2–3 minutes at rush hour.

## Trams and buses

Trams are the backbone of the city centre and the best way to see it. Buses serve the outer districts. At each stop (*zastávka*) a board shows the stop name, the lines and a timetable (*jízdní řád*); bigger stops have electronic departure boards (*odjezdy*).

Since 29 June 2024 **every bus and trolleybus stop** in Prague is a **request stop** (*zastávka na znamení*), and many tram stops are too: press the button inside the vehicle to get off. Buses now announce it as ***Pro výstup stiskněte tlačítko*** ("press the button to get off"). *(pid.cz)*

## At night

When the metro closes, **night trams 90–99** take over, and **night buses**, numbered from **901**, cover the rest of the city. *(pid.cz night timetables, checked 18 November 2026.)*

## To and from the airport

Václav Havel Airport is connected to the metro by:

- **line 59** to **Nádraží Veleslavín** (line A), about 35 minutes to the centre
- **bus 100** to **Zličín** (line B)
- **night lines 907 and 910** when the metro is closed

A **90-minute ticket** covers the whole trip, including the metro. The **Airport Express (AE)** bus to the main station is a separate service with its own fare: PID tickets aren't valid on it.

## Trains, ferries and the funicular

- **Trains** marked **S** (*esko*) within Prague accept PID tickets and are often the fastest way across the city, for example from the main station (*Hlavní nádraží*) to the outer districts.
- **Ferries** (*přívozy*) cross the Vltava at several points, mostly April to October. They're part of PID: hop on with your normal ticket.
- **The Petřín funicular** (*lanovka na Petřín*) climbs from Újezd to Petřín Hill. The 30- and 90-minute tickets **don't** cover it: a single ride is 100 Kč (90 Kč in the Lítačka app). 24-hour, 72-hour and monthly or yearly passes do. *(Checked on pid.cz, 17 November 2026.)*

## Accessibility

Most metro stations and almost all trams and buses are step-free (*bezbariérový*): look for the wheelchair symbol on timetables and in the app. Every vehicle has priority seats for older people, pregnant women and people with disabilities.
