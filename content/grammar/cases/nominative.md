## What it's used for

The nominative is the form you find in the dictionary. It answers **kdo? co?** (who? what?).

1. **The subject**: the person or thing doing the action.
   - ***Petr** pracuje v bance.* Petr works in a bank.
   - ***Káva** je studená.* The coffee is cold.
2. **After *to je / to jsou*** ("this is / these are"):
   - *To je **můj bratr**.* This is my brother.
   - *To jsou **moji rodiče**.* These are my parents.
3. **After *být* describing what someone is** (in everyday speech):
   - *Jsem **student**.* / *Jsem **studentka**.* I'm a student.

## Prepositions

The nominative is practically never used after prepositions. If you see a preposition, the noun after it is in another case.

## Compared with English

English "I, he, she, we, they" are nominative; "me, him, her, us, them" are not. Czech does the same thing, but with every noun, not only pronouns.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the nominative everywhere.** Beginners often say *do Praha* or *Dám si káva*. The nominative is right only for the subject; after verbs and prepositions, check which case they need.
- **Masculine animate plural.** People take special plural endings: *studenti, učitelé, páni/pánové*, and adjectives in **-í**: *dobří studenti*. Things don't: *dobré stroje*.
- **Formal "Je učitelem".** You'll read sentences like *Je učitelem* (instrumental). It means the same as *Je učitel*, but it's more formal or describes a role someone has taken on. In conversation, the nominative is fine.
