## What it's used for

The genitive answers **koho? čeho?** (of whom? of what?). It's one of the most frequent cases because so many common prepositions take it.

1. **Belonging, "of"** (where English uses *'s* or *of*). The genitive comes **after** the noun:
   - *auto **mého bratra*** my brother's car
   - *centrum **města*** the city centre
2. **Quantities and containers**:
   - *šálek **kávy*** a cup of coffee, *sklenice **vody*** a glass of water
   - *kilo **jablek*** a kilo of apples, *hodně **lidí*** a lot of people
3. **Numbers five and above** take the genitive plural:
   - *jedno pivo, dvě piva, tři piva*, but ***pět piv***
   - *dva roky*, but ***deset let***
4. **Dates**: *Je **prvního května**.* It's the first of May.
5. **After certain verbs**: *bát se* (be afraid of), *ptát se / zeptat se* (ask someone), *všimnout si* (notice):
   - *Bojím se **psů**.* I'm afraid of dogs.
   - *Zeptej se **paní**.* Ask the lady.

## Prepositions that take the genitive

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **do** | to, into | *Jedu **do Prahy**. Jdu **do kina**.* |
| **z / ze** | from, out of | *Jsem **z Anglie**. Káva **z automatu**.* |
| **od** | from (a person or point); since | *dopis **od mámy**, **od pondělí*** |
| **u** | at, by, at someone's place | *Jsem **u babičky**. **u okna*** |
| **bez** | without | *káva **bez cukru*** |
| **kromě** | except, apart from | ***kromě neděle*** |
| **během** | during | ***během týdne*** |
| **vedle** | next to | ***vedle nádraží*** |
| **podle** | according to; along | ***podle mě*** in my opinion |
| **kolem / okolo** | around, past | *Jdi **kolem kostela**.* |
| **blízko** | near | ***blízko centra*** |

**do vs. na:** *do* is for going *into* buildings, towns and countries: *do školy, do Brna*. Some places use *na* + accusative instead: *na poštu, na nádraží*. See the [locative](/grammar/locative) page for the matching "where" forms.

**z vs. od:** *z* means out of a place (*z Prahy, ze školy*); *od* means away from a person or the edge of something (*od doktora, od nádraží*).

## Compared with English

English uses "of" or *'s*: *the city centre*, *my brother's car*. Czech puts the owner in the genitive and **after** the thing: *auto bratra* (the car of brother). Czech has no word for "of"; the ending does the job.

## Common mistakes

- **Nominative after *do* and *z*.** *do Praha* → ***do Prahy***; *z Brno* → ***z Brna***.
- **Numbers 5+.** *pět pivo* → ***pět piv***; *deset korun* is genitive plural of *koruna*.
- **Genitive plural with no ending.** Feminine and neuter nouns often lose their ending entirely in the genitive plural: *žena → žen, město → měst, pivo → piv*. It looks wrong to English eyes but is very common.
- **Masculine -u vs -a.** Most inanimate masculine nouns take **-u** (*do obchodu, z hotelu*), but some common ones take **-a** (*do lesa, ze sýra, z chleba*). Learn these as you meet them.
