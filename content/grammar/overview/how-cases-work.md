## You already know cases

English has cases too, but only in its pronouns:

| Role | English | Czech |
|---|---|---|
| subject | **he** sees | **on** vidí |
| object | I see **him** | vidím **ho** |
| owner | **his** car | **jeho** auto |

English shows *who does what to whom* mostly by **word order**: "The dog bites the man" means something different from "The man bites the dog". Czech shows it with **endings**, so the word order is free to do other work (mostly to put new or important information at the end).

- *Pes kousl muž**e**.* The dog bit the man.
- *Muž**e** kousl pes.* It was a dog that bit the man. (Same facts, different emphasis.)

The ending **-e** on *muže* marks it as the object, wherever it stands.

## The seven cases

Czech schools number the cases 1 to 7 and teach them with question words. You'll see both in dictionaries and on Czech websites.

| # | Case | Question | Main job | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Nominative](/grammar/nominative) | kdo? co? | subject, dictionary form | **Káva** je dobrá. |
| 2 | [Genitive](/grammar/genitive) | koho? čeho? | "of", quantities, after *do, z, u, bez* | šálek **kávy** |
| 3 | [Dative](/grammar/dative) | komu? čemu? | "to/for someone", after *k, kvůli* | Dám to **mámě**. |
| 4 | [Accusative](/grammar/accusative) | koho? co? | direct object, after *na, pro, za* (+ motion) | Dám si **kávu**. |
| 5 | [Vocative](/grammar/vocative) | — | calling someone | **Petře**, pojď sem! |
| 6 | [Locative](/grammar/locative) | o kom? o čem? | only after *v, na, o, po, při* | v **Praze** |
| 7 | [Instrumental](/grammar/instrumental) | kým? čím? | "with", "by means of" | kafe s **mlékem** |

## Three questions decide the ending

To pick an ending you need to know:

1. **The case**, from the verb or preposition. *Do* always needs the genitive; *dát si* takes the accusative.
2. **The gender** of the noun: masculine (animate or inanimate), feminine or neuter. The last letter is a strong clue: most nouns in a consonant are masculine, in **-a** feminine, in **-o** neuter.
3. **Hard or soft**: whether the noun ends in a "soft" consonant (ž, š, č, ř, c, j, ď, ť, ň) or soft vowel (-e/-ě). Soft nouns have their own set of endings.

Each of these points to one **model word** (*pán, žena, město…*). Learn the model's table and every noun of the same type follows it. All 14 models are on the [tables page](/grammar/tables).

> **Tip:** don't try to memorise all the tables up front. Learn each case through the phrases you actually need (*do Prahy, v Praze, s mlékem*) and use the tables to check and see the pattern.
