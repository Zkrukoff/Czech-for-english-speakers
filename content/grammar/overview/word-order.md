## Free, but not random

Because Czech endings show who does what, the words can move. *Petr má rád Janu* and *Janu má rád Petr* both mean that Petr likes Jana (the endings say so). What changes is the **emphasis**.

Czech puts what's **new or important at the end** of the sentence. English does the same job with stress in your voice.

| Question | Answer | What's new |
|---|---|---|
| *Kde bydlí Petr?* Where does Petr live? | *Petr bydlí **v Brně**.* | where: in Brno |
| *Kdo bydlí v Brně?* Who lives in Brno? | *V Brně bydlí **Petr**.* | who: Petr |
| *Co si dáte?* What will you have? | *Dám si **kávu**.* | what: a coffee |
| *Dáte si kávu?* Will you have a coffee? | *Kávu si **dám**, ale bez mléka.* | yes, I will |

So when a sentence-building exercise accepts more than one order, they're all correct; they just put the weight on different words.

## The second position

Some short words are never stressed. They can't start a sentence, and they can't go just anywhere: they go in the **second position**, right after the first word or phrase. They're called *clitics* (*příklonky*).

- *jsem, jsi, jsme, jste* (in the past tense: *Čekal **jsem**.*) and *bych, bys, by, bychom, byste*
- *se, si*
- short pronouns: *mi, ti, mu, nám, vám, jim* (to me, to you, to him…) and *mě, tě, ho, ji, nás, vás, je* (me, you, him…)

> *Jmenuju **se** Sarah.*  My name is Sarah.
> *Jak **se** máš?*  How are you?
> *Moje kolegyně **se** jmenuje Emma.*  My colleague's name is Emma. (*Moje kolegyně* is the first phrase.)
> *Už **ho** vidím.*  I can see him now.

## When several come together

They keep a fixed order, always:

1. *jsem / bych…*
2. *se / si*
3. *mi, ti, mu…* (to whom)
4. *mě, tě, ho…* (whom)

> *Koupil **jsem si** jízdenku.*  I bought (myself) a ticket.
> *Já **bych si** dal řízek.*  I'd have the schnitzel.
> *Jak **se ti** líbí nová práce?*  How do you like the new job?
> *Dal **jsem mu ho**.*  I gave it to him.

## The usual traps

- **Starting with the little word.** *~~Se jmenuju Sarah.~~* → *Jmenuju se Sarah.*
- **Putting the pronoun at the end, English style.** *~~Vidím už ho.~~* → *Už ho vidím.* · *~~Kolegové pomáhají mi.~~* → *Kolegové mi pomáhají.*
- **The wrong order in a group.** *~~Koupil si jsem…~~* → *Koupil jsem si…* · *~~Jak ti se líbí…~~* → *Jak se ti líbí…*
- ***jsem* with he, she and they.** In the past tense, *jsem* is only for I, you, we and you-all: *Byl jsem doma* (I was at home), but *Byl doma* (he was at home), never *~~Byl je doma~~*.

In fast speech you'll hear exceptions, and nobody will mind if you slip. The pattern is worth getting right, because it's the thing that makes you sound like you speak Czech rather than translate into it.
