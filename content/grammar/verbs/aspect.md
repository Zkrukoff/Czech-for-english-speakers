## Two verbs for one English verb

English "to write" corresponds to **two** Czech verbs:

- ***psát*** (imperfective): writing as an activity, a process, a habit
- ***napsat*** (perfective): writing something **to completion**, with a result

This is called **aspect**, and almost every Czech verb belongs to a pair. When you learn a verb, learn its partner too. The [verb lookup](/verbs/lookup) shows each verb's partner.

The question to ask yourself is: ***Am I talking about the activity, or about a single complete result?***

## Imperfective: the process, the habit, the general fact

Use the imperfective verb when you are talking about:

1. **What's happening right now** (the present tense is *only* imperfective):
   - *Co děláš? — **Píšu** e-mail.* What are you doing? — I'm writing an e-mail.
2. **Habits and repeated actions**:
   - *Každé ráno **piju** kávu.* I drink coffee every morning.
   - *Často jsme **jezdili** na hory.* We often used to go to the mountains.
3. **An activity, without saying whether it was finished**:
   - *Včera večer jsem **četl**.* I was reading last night. / I spent last night reading.
4. **How long something lasted**:
   - *Dvě hodiny jsem **psal** dopis.* I spent two hours writing a letter.
5. **General statements and questions about whether something happens at all**:
   - ***Kouříte**?* Do you smoke?
6. **After *začít, přestat, skončit*** (start, stop, finish). These are always followed by an imperfective infinitive:
   - *Začal jsem se **učit** česky.* I started learning Czech.

## Perfective: one completed action, a result

Use the perfective verb when you are talking about:

1. **A single action that was (or will be) completed**:
   - *Včera jsem **napsal** dopis.* I wrote a letter yesterday (and it's finished).
   - ***Přečetla** jsi tu knihu?* Did you read (finish) that book?
2. **A sequence of events**, one after another:
   - ***Vstal** jsem, **osprchoval** se a **šel** do práce.* I got up, had a shower and went to work.
3. **The future**: the "present" forms of a perfective verb mean the **future**:
   - ***Napíšu** ti zítra.* I'll write to you tomorrow.
   - ***Koupím** chleba.* I'll buy some bread.
   - ***Zaplatím**, prosím.* I'd like to pay, please. (Literally: I will pay.)

**A perfective verb can never describe something happening right now.** *Napíšu* is always future.

## Side by side

| Imperfective (process, habit) | Perfective (one completed result) |
|---|---|
| *Co **děláš**?* What are you doing? | *Co **uděláš**?* What will you do (about it)? |
| *Každý den **kupuju** rohlíky.* I buy rolls every day. | ***Koupím** rohlíky.* I'll buy (some) rolls. |
| *Celý den jsem **vařila**.* I was cooking all day. | ***Uvařila** jsem večeři.* I cooked (made) dinner. |
| *Ráda **piju** víno.* I like drinking wine. | ***Vypil** jsem celou láhev.* I drank the whole bottle. |
| ***Volal** jsem ti několikrát.* I called you several times. | ***Zavolám** ti večer.* I'll call you tonight. |
| ***Čekám** na autobus.* I'm waiting for the bus. | ***Počkej** chvilku!* Wait a moment! |

## How pairs are formed

There are three main patterns:

1. **A prefix makes the perfective** (most common): *psát → **na**psat, dělat → **u**dělat, číst → **pře**číst, pít → **vy**pít, platit → **za**platit, volat → **za**volat, vařit → **u**vařit*.
2. **A suffix changes** (the imperfective is usually longer): *kupovat → koupit, dávat → dát, vstávat → vstát, začínat → začít, otvírat → otevřít*.
3. **Completely different verbs**: *brát → vzít* (take), *říkat → říct* (say).

Unfortunately the prefix isn't predictable: you have to learn which one each verb uses.

> **Prefixes can also change the meaning.** *psát* (write) → *napsat* (write, perfective) but *přepsat* (rewrite), *podepsat* (sign). Those new verbs then form their own imperfectives: *přepisovat, podepisovat*.

## Aspect and the tenses

| | Imperfective | Perfective |
|---|---|---|
| Present | ***píšu*** I write / I'm writing | (doesn't exist) |
| Past | ***psal jsem*** I wrote / was writing | ***napsal jsem*** I wrote (finished) |
| Future | ***budu psát*** I'll be writing | ***napíšu*** I'll write (finish) |

See the [future tense](/verbs/future) for the details.

## Aspect in commands

- **Positive commands** are usually **perfective**: ***Zavři** okno!* Close the window!
- **Negative commands** are usually **imperfective**: ***Nezavírej** okno!* Don't close the window!

More on this on the [imperative](/verbs/imperative) page.

## How English speakers can think about it

English "I was writing" (progressive) is always imperfective, and "I have written / I wrote it (finished)" is usually perfective. But English simple past "I wrote" can be either, so you have to ask: *was it one complete result?*

A useful test: **could you add "and finished it" or "once and for all"?** If yes, the perfective is likely. If you could add "for a while", "regularly" or "at that moment", use the imperfective.

## Common mistakes

- ***Budu napsat*** → ***Napíšu*** (or ***Budu psát***). *Budu* never goes with a perfective verb.
- ***Teď napíšu e-mail*** meaning "I'm writing an e-mail now" → ***Teď píšu e-mail.*** (*Teď napíšu* means "I'll write it now", i.e. right away.)
- ***Začal jsem napsat*** → ***Začal jsem psát.***
- **Habits with the perfective**: ***Každý den koupím noviny*** → ***Každý den kupuju noviny.***
