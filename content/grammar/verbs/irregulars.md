## být: to be

The most important verb in Czech, and completely irregular.

```verb být
```

- *Jsem z Anglie.* I'm from England.
- *Kde **jsi**?* Where are you?
- *To **je** dobré.* That's good.

**Negative:** *nejsem, nejsi, **není**, nejsme, nejste, nejsou*. Note *není* (isn't), not *neje*.

The forms *jsem, jsi, jsme, jste* are **clitics**: unstressed little words that sit in the **second position** of the sentence. *Jsem doma.* but *Doma **jsem** v šest.* This matters a lot in the [past tense](/verbs/past).

More on where these little words go: [Word order and the little words](/grammar/word-order).

> **Heard on the street:** *seš* instead of *jsi*: *Kde seš?*

## mít: to have

Regular class-5 endings, but a short stem.

```verb mít
```

- *Máš čas?* Do you have time?
- *Nemám hlad.* I'm not hungry. (Literally: I don't have hunger.)

Czech uses *mít* in many phrases where English uses *be*: *mít hlad* (be hungry), *mít žízeň* (be thirsty), *mít pravdu* (be right), *mít rád* (to like / love): *Mám rád kávu.*

## chtít: to want

```verb chtít
```

- *Chci kávu.* I want coffee. (Direct, fine with friends.)
- *Chtěl bych kávu.* I'd like a coffee. (Polite; see the [conditional](/verbs/conditional).)

## jít: to go (on foot)

```verb jít
```

The past tense is irregular: ***šel, šla, šli*** (he went, she went, they went). The future is ***půjdu*** (never *budu jít*). For when to use *jít* vs. *jet* and *chodit*, see [verbs of motion](/verbs/motion).

## vědět: to know (a fact)

```verb vědět
```

*Nevím.* (I don't know.) is one of the most useful words you'll learn. Don't confuse *vědět* (know a fact: *Vím, kde to je*) with *znát* (know a person or place: *Znám Prahu*).

## Common mistakes

- ***Já jsem mám…*** → ***Mám…*** *Být* isn't used with other verbs in the present.
- ***neje*** → ***není***.
- ***Mám dvacet let*** → ***Je mi dvacet let.*** Age uses *být* + dative, not *mít*.
- ***Jsem hladový*** is understood but unusual → ***Mám hlad.***
