## What se and si are

*se* and *si* mean "oneself": ***se*** is the accusative (myself as the object) and ***si*** is the dative (to/for myself). The same word is used for every person:

- *Jmenuju **se** Anna.* (I call myself Anna.) *Jak **se** jmenuješ?*
- *Dám **si** kávu.* (I give to myself a coffee = I'll have a coffee.) *Co **si** dáte?*

## Learn the verb with its se or si

Often there's no "oneself" in the English translation at all. The *se/si* is just part of the verb:

| with se | | with si | |
|---|---|---|---|
| jmenovat se | be called | dát si | have (food/drink) |
| učit se | learn, study | koupit si | buy (for oneself) |
| bát se | be afraid | myslet si | think (an opinion) |
| dívat se | look, watch | sednout si | sit down |
| ptát se | ask | lehnout si | lie down |
| líbit se | please (= like) | pamatovat si | remember |
| těšit se | look forward | odpočinout si | have a rest |
| smát se | laugh | vzpomenout si | recall |

## se/si can change the meaning

| without | with se/si |
|---|---|
| *učit* teach: *Učím angličtinu.* | *učit se* learn: *Učím se češtinu.* |
| *jmenovat* name, appoint | *jmenovat se* be called |
| *myslet (na)* think (about) | *myslet si* have an opinion: *Myslím si, že…* |
| *mýt* wash (something) | *mýt se* wash (oneself) |

## Position: always second

*se* and *si* are clitics: they go in the **second position** of the clause, after the first word or phrase. They don't stick to the verb as in English "wash *myself*":

- ***Jmenuju se** Tom.*
- *Já **se** jmenuju Tom.*
- *Jak **se** jmenuje tvoje sestra?*
- *Zítra **si** koupím nové boty.*

In the past tense they come after *jsem/jsi…*: *Včera **jsem se** učil.* With *ty*: ***ses**, **sis***: *Co **sis** dal?*

## Other uses of se

- **"Each other"**: *Známe **se**.* We know each other. *Uvidíme **se** zítra.* See you tomorrow.
- **General statements** (English "you / one / people"): *Jak **se** to řekne česky?* How do you say it in Czech? *Tady **se** nekouří.* No smoking here.

## Common mistakes

- ***Jmenuju Tom*** → ***Jmenuju se Tom.*** Leaving out *se* changes or breaks the meaning.
- ***Se jmenuju Tom*** → ***Jmenuju se Tom.*** *se* can't come first.
- ***Učím česky*** (I teach Czech) when you mean "I'm learning Czech" → ***Učím se česky.***
- **se vs si**: *dát se* means something else entirely (e.g. *dát se do práce*, start working). For ordering it's always ***dát si***.
