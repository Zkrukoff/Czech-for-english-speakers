## How modals work

As in English, a modal verb is followed by an **infinitive**:

- ***Můžu** platit kartou?* Can I pay by card?
- ***Musím** jít.* I have to go.
- ***Chci** spát.* I want to sleep.

## The six modals

| Verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **moct** | can, may (be able / allowed) | *Můžete mi pomoct?* Can you help me? |
| **muset** | must, have to | *Musím vstávat v šest.* I have to get up at six. |
| **chtít** | want | *Chceš jít ven?* Do you want to go out? |
| **smět** | be allowed to, may | *Smím dál?* May I come in? |
| **mít** | be supposed to, should | *Mám zavolat?* Should I call? |
| **umět** | know how to, can (a skill) | *Umíš plavat?* Can you swim? |

## moct vs. umět

English "can" covers both, Czech doesn't:

- ***umět*** = a learned skill: *Umím řídit.* I can drive (I know how). *Umím trochu česky.* I speak a little Czech.
- ***moct*** = possibility or permission: *Dnes nemůžu řídit, piju.* I can't drive today, I'm drinking.

## The big trap: nemusím vs. nesmím

| Czech | English | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *Nemusíš to dělat.* | You **don't have to** do it. | It's not necessary. |
| *Nesmíš to dělat.* | You **mustn't** do it. | It's forbidden. |

English "must not" looks like *nemusím*, but means *nesmím*. Signs: ***Kouření zakázáno*** / ***Tady se nesmí kouřit.***

## mít as "should"

- *Co **mám** dělat?* What should I do?
- ***Měl bys** jít k doktorovi.* You should go to the doctor. (conditional, softer advice; see [conditional](/verbs/conditional))

## Common mistakes

- ***Nemusíš kouřit tady*** (meant as "you mustn't") → ***Tady nesmíš kouřit.***
- ***Můžu plavat*** (meaning "I know how to swim") → ***Umím plavat.***
- ***Musím, že…*** Modals take an infinitive, not a *that*-clause: *Musím jít.*
- **mohu / můžu**: both are correct. *Můžu* is neutral; *mohu* sounds more formal.
