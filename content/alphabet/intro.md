## Read it as it's written

Czech spelling is very regular. Once you know what each letter sounds like, you can read any word aloud, including street names, station announcements, your colleagues' surnames and the label on a medicine box. The alphabet has **42 letters**: the 26 you know, plus letters with **diacritics** and the two-character letter **ch**.

| Mark | Name | What it does | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| ´ | **čárka** | makes a vowel **long** (never shows stress) | á é í ó ú ý |
| ˚ | **kroužek** | only on ů: the same long sound as ú | ů |
| ˇ | **háček** | changes a consonant (or ě) | č š ž ř ď ť ň ě |

On ď and ť the háček is written like an apostrophe (ď, ť), but the capitals are Ď and Ť.

## Five rules that cover almost everything

1. **Stress is always on the first syllable.** *O*-lo-mouc, *Pra*-ha, *dě*-ku-ju. The čárka doesn't show stress.
2. **Vowel length matters.** Long vowels take about twice as long, and changing the length can change the word: *pas* (passport) / *pás* (belt), *byt* (flat) / *být* (to be).
3. **di, ti, ni are soft.** Before *i, í, ě*, the letters d, t, n are pronounced ď, ť, ň: *dívka* [ďívka], *ticho* [ťicho], *nic* [ňic]. Before y they stay hard: *ty* [ty]. (Many borrowed words are exceptions: *diplom, tým*.)
4. **ě changes the consonant before it.** *bě, pě, vě, fě* → bje, pje, vje, fje; *dě, tě, ně* → ďe, ťe, ňe; *mě* → mňe (*město* [mňesto]).
5. **Consonants at the end of a word lose their voice.** *hrad* [hrat], *chléb* [chlép], *sníh* [sňích]. The same happens before another voiceless consonant: *všechno* [fšechno].

And one surprise: **r and l can be vowels.** Words like *Brno, vlk* (wolf) and *čtvrtek* (Thursday) have a syllable with no vowel at all. The famous test: *Strč prst skrz krk.*

## Sounds that trip up English speakers

| Letter | Say it like… | Not like… |
|---|---|---|
| **c** | "ts" in *cats* | "c" in *cat* |
| **ch** | "ch" in Scottish *loch* | "ch" in *church* (that's **č**) |
| **j** | "y" in *yes* | "j" in *jam* |
| **h** | a voiced, buzzing h | silent or breathy h |
| **r** | a tapped r, as in Scottish English | the English r |
| **ř** | r + ž at the same time | r or ž alone |
| **y** | exactly like **i** | "y" in *yes* (that's **j**) |
| **p, t, k** | without the puff of air | English *pin, tin, kin* |

## Spelling on the phone

At the foreign police, the bank, the doctor's or on a customer-service line, you'll often have to spell your name or e-mail address. Czechs name each letter (*bé, cé, dé…*) and add the diacritic: *á s čárkou, c s háčkem, u s kroužkem*. To be extra clear, they use the traditional **phone-spelling alphabet**: *D jako David, Č jako Čeněk*.

Useful words:

- **zavináč**: @ (in e-mail addresses)
- **tečka**: dot · **pomlčka**: hyphen · **podtržítko**: underscore
- **velké / malé písmeno**: capital / small letter
- **bez diakritiky**: without diacritics (as most e-mail addresses are)

> **Tip for expats:** practise spelling your own name and e-mail address the Czech way until it's automatic. It's one of the most-used skills in your first months.
