## Two ways to say "you"

Czech has two words for *you*, and choosing one is a social decision, not just grammar.

| | **vy** (vykání) | **ty** (tykání) |
|---|---|---|
| Who | strangers, shop and office staff, older people, your boss, your landlord | friends, family, children, anyone who has offered *ty* |
| Hello | *Dobrý den* | *Ahoj, Čau* |
| How are you? | *Jak se **máte**?* | *Jak se **máš**?* |
| Where are you from? | *Odkud **jste**?* | *Odkud **jsi**?* |
| Coffee? | ***Dáte** si kafe?* | ***Dáš** si kafe?* |
| That's kind of you | *To je od **vás** milé.* | *To je od **tebe** milé.* |

## The verb follows

With **vy**, the verb is in the **2nd person plural**, even when you're talking to one person: *Odkud jste?* With **ty**, it's 2nd person singular: *Odkud jsi?* You already know both forms from the present tense; the only new thing is choosing.

## Names go with it

*Vy* usually goes with a title and surname: *pane Nováku, paní Kovářová*. *Ty* goes with a first name in the vocative: *Ondro, Jano, Petře*. A first name with *vy* exists too (colleagues who aren't close yet), but it's a halfway house.

## When in doubt

Use *vy*. Nobody is offended by politeness; plenty of people are put off by *ty* from a stranger. At work, start with *vy* and wait to be offered the switch (next lesson).
