## Introducing people

- ***To je*** *Pavel, náš vývojář.* This is Pavel, our developer.
- ***Tohle je*** *paní Horáková.* This (here) is Mrs Horáková. (*tohle* points a little more: "this one here")
- ***Kdo je to?*** / ***Kdo to je?*** Who's that?

## Jobs

| man | woman |
|---|---|
| vývojář | vývojář**ka** |
| asistent | asistent**ka** |
| ředitel | ředitel**ka** |
| manažer | manažer**ka** |
| účetní | účetní |
| vedoucí | vedoucí |

*Účetní* (accountant) and *vedoucí* (manager, head) are adjectives used as nouns: the same word for a man or a woman, declined like *jarní*.

## Saying what someone does

- ***Pracuje jako*** *asistentka.* She works as an assistant. (*jako* + nominative)
- ***Je*** *vývojář.* He's a developer. (no word for "a")
- ***Má na starosti*** *faktury.* She's in charge of invoices. (+ accusative)
- ***Pracuje v*** *marketingu / v IT / v účetním oddělení.* She works in marketing / in IT / in accounts. (*v* + [locative](/grammar/locative))

You'll also read sentences like *Je ředitelem firmy* (instrumental). It means the same as *Je ředitel firmy*, but is more formal: typical of e-mails, websites and introductions at events.

## Ty or vy at work?

Many Czech workplaces, especially international companies and IT, use *ty* among colleagues from day one. Traditional offices, public institutions and older colleagues often stay with *vy* for a long time. The usual rule: **the more senior person, or the woman, offers *ty*** (*Můžeme si tykat?* Shall we use ty?). Until then, use *vy* with *Dobrý den*, and follow your colleagues' lead.
