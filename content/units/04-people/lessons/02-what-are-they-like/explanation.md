## Jaký je? What's he like?

Czech separates two questions English sometimes blurs:

- ***Jak vypadá?*** What does he/she look like?
- ***Jaký je?*** / ***Jaká je?*** What's he/she like (as a person)?

*Jaký* is an adjective, so it agrees: *jaký šéf, jaká šéfová, jaké oddělení*.

## Describing character

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| milý (nice, kind) | přísný (strict) | pracovitý (hard-working) |
| sympatický (likeable) | spravedlivý (fair) | náročný (demanding) |
| vtipný (funny) | klidný (calm) | líný (lazy) |

Make it stronger or softer: ***velmi*** / ***moc*** (very), ***docela*** (quite), ***trochu*** (a bit), ***vůbec ne*** (not at all). *Je trochu přísný, ale spravedlivý.*

> **False friend:** *sympatický* means likeable, pleasant, someone you take to. It does **not** mean "sympathetic" (that's *soucitný* or *chápavý*).

## Groups of people: -í

For plural **masculine animate** nouns (men, or a mixed group), adjectives end in **-í**, and the consonant before it often softens:

| singular | plural (people) |
|---|---|
| milý kolega | mil**í** kolegové |
| přísný šéf | přísn**í** šéfové |
| sympatický | sympati**čtí** |
| chytrý (clever) | chyt**ří** |

For women and things the plural is **-é**: *milé kolegyně, nové počítače*. See the [nominative](/grammar/nominative) page.

## Likes and help: the dative

*Jak **se ti** líbí nová práce?* How do you like the new job? (literally "How does the new job please you?") and *pomáhají **mi** s češtinou* (they help me with Czech) both use the [dative](/grammar/dative): the person who is pleased or helped.
