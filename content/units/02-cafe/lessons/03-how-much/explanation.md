## Asking the price

- ***Kolik to stojí?*** How much is it?
- ***Kolik stojí** ty rohlíky?* How much are those rolls?
- ***Kolik to bude?*** / ***Kolik to dělá?*** How much will that be? (at the till)

## Numbers 1–100

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 jeden | 6 šest | 11 jedenáct | 20 dvacet |
| 2 dva | 7 sedm | 12 dvanáct | 30 třicet |
| 3 tři | 8 osm | 13 třináct | 40 čtyřicet |
| 4 čtyři | 9 devět | 15 patnáct | 50 padesát |
| 5 pět | 10 deset | 19 devatenáct | 100 sto |

Combine tens and units in order: *dvacet pět* (25), *čtyřicet osm* (48). You'll also hear the old style **pětadvacet** (five-and-twenty), especially from older speakers. Both are correct.

*Jeden* and *dva* change with gender: *jeden rohlík, jedna koruna, jedno pivo*; *dva rohlíky, dvě koruny, dvě piva*.

## Numbers change the noun after them

This pattern is used all the time with prices:

| Number | Case of the noun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | nominative singular | jedna korun**a**, jeden rohlík |
| 2, 3, 4 | nominative plural | dvě korun**y**, tři rohlík**y** |
| 5 and up | **genitive plural** | pět korun, deset rohlík**ů** |

So you'll see prices written like *1 koruna, 3 koruny, 80 korun*. *Korun* has no ending at all: feminine nouns often lose it in the genitive plural.

Words of quantity work like big numbers: ***kolik** rohlíků?, **hodně** lidí* (a lot of people).

## Paying

- *Můžu platit **kartou**?* Can I pay by card?
- *Platím **hotově**.* I'm paying in cash.
