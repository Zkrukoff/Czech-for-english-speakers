## The accusative: the thing you want

In English, "coffee" looks the same whether it's the subject ("Coffee is hot") or the object ("I'll have coffee"). Czech marks the **object** of a verb with a different ending. This is the **accusative case** (4. pád).

When you order, the thing you order is the object of **dát si** ("to have, to treat yourself to"):

| Dictionary form (nominative) | After *Dám si…* (accusative) |
|---|---|
| káv**a** | Dám si káv**u**. |
| vod**a** | Dám si vod**u**. |
| čaj | Dám si čaj. |
| pivo | Dám si pivo. |

**The rule for now:** feminine nouns ending in **-a** change it to **-u**. Masculine *inanimate* nouns (things, like *čaj*) and neuter nouns (like *pivo*) don't change at all.

## Why "si"?

*Dát* means "to give". *Dát si* is literally "to give (to) oneself", which is how Czech says "to have" when ordering. The little word **si** goes in the second position of the sentence: *Dám **si** kávu. Co **si** dáte?*

> **Heard on the street:** many people say **kafe** instead of *káva*. *Kafe* is neuter and never changes: *Dám si kafe.*
