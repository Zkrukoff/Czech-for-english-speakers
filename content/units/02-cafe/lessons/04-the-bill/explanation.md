## Asking for the bill

| You say | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ***Zaplatím, prosím.*** | I'd like to pay, please. (lit. "I'll pay") |
| ***Zaplatíme, prosím.*** | We'd like to pay, please. |
| ***Účet, prosím.*** | The bill, please. |

To catch a waiter's attention: ***Pane vrchní!*** (traditional, still common in pubs) or just ***Prosím vás…*** (Excuse me…).

The waiter will often ask: ***Dohromady, nebo zvlášť?*** (Together or separately?) and ***Platíte kartou, nebo hotově?*** (Card or cash?).

## Why "zaplatím" means "I'll pay"

*zaplatit* is a **perfective** verb: it describes one complete action (paying the bill). Perfective verbs have **no present tense**, so their present forms refer to the future:

- ***Zaplatím.*** I'll pay. / I'd like to pay.
- *Kdy **zaplatíš**?* When will you pay?

Its imperfective partner *platit* is for the process or a habit:

- ***Platíte** kartou?* Are you paying by card?
- *Vždycky **platím** kartou.* I always pay by card.

This **aspect** pair is one of the most important ideas in Czech. Read the [aspect](/verbs/aspect) page when you're ready.

## Tipping

Tips (**spropitné**) are usually about 10 %. Rather than leaving money on the table, you tell the waiter the total you want to pay: for a bill of 460 Kč, hand over 500 and say ***To je v pořádku*** (keep the change), or say ***Pět set, prosím*** before paying by card.

> **Heard on the street:** *To je dobrý.* (keep the change). Standard Czech would be *to je dobré*; *dobrý* is the spoken form (obecná čeština).

## Past tense preview: bylo to…

*Bylo to výborné.* It was excellent. *Chutnalo vám?* Did you enjoy it (lit. "did it taste good to you")? These use the [past tense](/verbs/past); learn them as phrases for now.
