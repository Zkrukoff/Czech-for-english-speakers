## Buying a ticket

- ***Jednu jízdenku do Brna, prosím.*** One ticket to Brno, please.
- ***Jednosměrnou, nebo zpáteční?*** Single or return?
- ***Zpáteční, prosím.*** Return, please.

*Jízdenka* is in the **accusative** (*jízdenku*) because it's the thing you're asking for, even though "I'd like" isn't said. The number agrees too: *jedna → jednu*. *Lístek* (masculine inanimate) doesn't change: *jeden lístek do Brna*.

## Asking about times

- ***V kolik** jede vlak do Brna?* What time is the train to Brno?
- ***Kdy** odjíždí autobus?* When does the bus leave?
- ***Musím přestupovat?*** Do I have to change?
- *Ten vlak jede **přímo**.* That train goes direct.

## Telling the time

Full hours use **v** + the number (in the accusative, which mostly looks the same):

| | |
|---|---|
| v jednu | at one |
| ve dvě, ve tři, ve čtyři | at two, three, four |
| v pět, v deset | at five, at ten |

**Half hours count towards the next hour**. This is a classic trap for English speakers:

| Czech | Literally | Means |
|---|---|---|
| v půl desáté | at half (to) ten | **9:30** |
| v půl jedenácté | at half (to) eleven | **10:30** |
| v půl jedné | at half (to) one | **12:30** |

Timetables and announcements use the 24-hour clock: *v deset dvacet* (10:20), *ve čtrnáct třicet* (14:30).

## odjíždět / odjet

Train announcements and timetables use the imperfective **odjíždět** (leave, depart): *Vlak **odjíždí** v deset.* Its perfective partner **odjet** is for a single completed departure: *Vlak už **odjel**.* The train has already left.
