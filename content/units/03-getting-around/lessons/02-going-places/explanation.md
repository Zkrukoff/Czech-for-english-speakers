## jít or jet?

English "go" doesn't say how you travel. Czech does:

| | on foot | by vehicle (car, bus, train, tram…) |
|---|---|---|
| one trip, now | **jít**: *Jdu do práce.* | **jet**: *Jedu do Brna.* |
| habit, regularly | **chodit**: *Chodím do práce pěšky.* | **jezdit**: *Jezdím do práce autobusem.* |

```verb jet
```

Use *jet* even if someone else is driving: *Jedu tramvají.* Saying *Jdu do Brna* would mean you're walking there!

The future of *jít* and *jet* is ***půjdu*** and ***pojedu***: *Pojedu tramvají.* I'll take the tram. See [verbs of motion](/verbs/motion) for more.

## Where to? do + genitive, na + accusative

To say where you're going, use **do** or **na**:

| do + genitive (into a place) | na + accusative (some places, events) |
|---|---|
| do Brna, do Prahy | na nádraží |
| do kina | na poštu |
| do práce | na koncert |
| do školy | na víkend (for the weekend) |
| do obchodu | na náměstí |

Most buildings, towns and countries take **do**. Open spaces, events and some institutions take **na**. Learn each place with its preposition.

And one special word: ***domů*** = (to) home. *Jdu domů.*

## How? The instrumental for transport

The means of transport goes in the **instrumental**, without a preposition:

| | by… |
|---|---|
| vlak | vlak**em** |
| autobus | autobus**em** |
| auto | aut**em** |
| tramvaj | tramvaj**í** |
| metro | metr**em** |

*pěšky* = on foot.
