## Odkud jsi? Jsem z…

To say where you're from, use **z** ("from, out of") and change the ending of the place name. After *z*, Czech always uses the **genitive case** (2. pád).

| Place | From… |
|---|---|
| Kanad**a** | z Kanad**y** |
| Prah**a** | z Prah**y** |
| Brn**o** | z Brn**a** |
| Česk**o** | z Česk**a** |
| Angli**e** | z Angli**e** |
| Londýn | z Londýn**a** |

The pattern for now:

- feminine **-a → -y**: *Amerika → z Ameriky*
- neuter **-o → -a**: *Brno → z Brna*
- feminine **-e** stays **-e**: *Anglie → z Anglie, Francie → z Francie*
- masculine (consonant) **+ -a** or **+ -u**: *z Londýna*, but *z Manchesteru*. You'll learn which as you go.

Before some consonant groups, **z** becomes **ze**: *ze Skotska, ze Slovenska*.

Don't try to learn the whole genitive now. Just learn your own country and city, and the ones you hear most. For the full picture, see the [genitive](/grammar/genitive).

## Jobs and nationalities: two forms

Czech words for people usually have a masculine and a feminine form. The feminine often ends in **-ka**:

| He is… | She is… |
|---|---|
| učitel | učitel**ka** |
| student | student**ka** |
| Angličan | Angličan**ka** |
| Čech | Češ**ka** |
| programátor | programátor**ka** |

And there's no word for "a/an": *Jsem učitelka.* I'm a teacher.

## Where you live: bydlet v…

*Bydlím **v Praze**.* I live in Prague. After *v* (in), the place name changes again (*Praha → v Praze*). That's the locative case, coming up in unit 3. For now, just learn your own city as a phrase.
