## Two ways to say "you"

Czech has two words for "you", and choosing the right one is part of being polite:

| | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **ty** | friends, family, children, people your age in casual settings | *Jak se **máš**?* |
| **vy** | strangers, older people, shop staff, colleagues you don't know well, your boss | *Jak se **máte**?* |

Using *ty* is called **tykání**, using *vy* is **vykání**. When in doubt, use *vy*: it's always safe, and Czechs will suggest switching to *ty* when they're ready. *Vy* is also the plural "you" for a group.

## Greetings by situation

| Situation | Hello | Goodbye |
|---|---|---|
| formal (vy) | **Dobrý den.** / Dobré ráno. / Dobrý večer. | **Na shledanou.** |
| informal (ty) | **Ahoj.** / Čau. | **Ahoj.** / Čau. / Měj se. |

*Dobrý den* works all day long. *Dobré ráno* is for early morning and *Dobrý večer* for the evening. *Dobrou noc* (good night) is only said when going to bed.

## How are you?

The question uses the verb **mít se**, literally "to have oneself":

- ***Jak se máš?*** (ty) / ***Jak se máte?*** (vy)
- ***Dobře**, díky.* Fine, thanks. / *Ujde to.* Not bad. / *Nic moc.* Not great.
- ***A ty?** / **A vy?*** And you?

Czechs tend to answer this question honestly. *Ujde to* or *Nic moc* are perfectly normal answers.

## Names change when you speak to someone

You may have noticed *Ahoj, Jan**o**!* and *Ahoj, Tomáš**i**!* in the dialogue. Jana's and Tomáš's names change because someone is speaking *to* them. This is the **vocative**, and you'll practise it in lesson 4.
