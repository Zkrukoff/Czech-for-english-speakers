## být: to be

```verb být
```

Because the verb ending already shows who you mean, Czech usually **leaves out the pronoun**:

- ***Jsem** Martin.* I'm Martin.
- ***Jsi** tady nová?* Are you new here?

Adding *já, ty…* gives emphasis or contrast, as in introductions: *Ahoj, **já** jsem Martin. A **ty**?*

## jmenovat se: to be called

To give your name, Czech says "I call myself":

- ***Jmenuju se** Anna.* My name is Anna.
- *Jak **se jmenuješ**?* What's your name? (ty)
- *Jak **se jmenujete**?* What's your name? (vy)
- *Jak **se jmenuje**?* What's his/her name?

The little word **se** ("oneself") must be in the **second position** in the sentence, never first:

- *Jmenuju **se** Anna.* ✓
- *Já **se** jmenuju Anna.* ✓
- ~~*Se jmenuju Anna.*~~ ✗

You'll also hear *jmenuji* instead of *jmenuju*. Both are correct; *jmenuju* is more common in speech.

## Nice to meet you

- ***Těší mě.*** Nice to meet you. (Literally "it pleases me".)
- ***Mě taky.*** Likewise. (Literally "me too": "it pleases me too".)

## Male and female forms

Many words for people have two forms. Sarah is *nová* (new, feminine); Martin would be *nový*. A female friend is a *kamarádka*, a male friend a *kamarád*. You'll see more of this in the next lesson.
